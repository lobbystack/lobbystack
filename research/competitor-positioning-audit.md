# Competitor positioning audit

Checked: 2026-07-30

Scope: the 12 English competitor posts and their research dossiers. This audit identifies LobbyStack advantages that the posts omit or bury, plus price framing that could mislead a buyer. It does not treat calls, callers, interactions, credits, and minutes as equivalent units.

## LobbyStack baseline to use in every comparison

| Plan or capability | Publishable fact |
| --- | --- |
| Free | $0, no credit card, 30 voice minutes, 10 alert SMS segments, 2 outbound call attempts, 25 MB knowledge base, and all product features |
| Starter | $30 monthly or $24 monthly with annual billing; 150 voice minutes, then $0.20/min; 50 alert SMS segments; 20 outbound call attempts |
| Pro | $100 monthly or $80 monthly with annual billing; 500 voice minutes, then $0.18/min; 200 alert SMS segments; 100 outbound call attempts |
| Included on all plans | Appointment booking, Google and Outlook calendars, appointment confirmations, appointment changes, transfers, urgent handoff, email/SMS notifications, summaries, transcripts, recordings, call history, spam filtering, website knowledge import, multilingual support, and concurrent calls |
| Deployment | Managed cloud for low-ops setup; public AGPL-3.0 source and self-hosting for teams that want to control provider accounts, infrastructure, retention, upgrades, and custom integrations |
| Honest boundary | LobbyStack hands calls to the customer's configured team. It does not bundle an external live-receptionist workforce. Self-hosting adds operations, security, backup, and upgrade work. |

The existing posts mention source access and self-hosting well. They underuse the ongoing free plan, the all-features policy, included SMS allowances, unlimited booking, and unlimited call records. These facts distinguish LobbyStack from trials, feature-gated entry plans, and products that require a phone-suite subscription.

## Highest-priority corrections

1. Fix Frontdesk's “close at 200 minutes” sentence. The published LobbyStack Starter cost at 200 minutes is $40 monthly before other metered usage; Frontdesk is $99 monthly and includes a broader bundle.
2. Reframe Quo's “free” allowance as a $0 Sona add-on allowance on a paid Quo plan.
3. Remove the implied Zoom Phone or Workplace prerequisite from the standalone Zoom comparison.
4. Separate Smith.ai's included AI-to-human escalation from live-agent tasks sold as per-call add-ons.
5. Correct the Rosie tier names and keep in-call texting distinct from alert/follow-up SMS.
6. Add LobbyStack's ongoing no-card Free plan and all-features policy to every table where the competitor presents a trial, free allowance, or feature-gated entry tier.

## Reusable fair-positioning rules

- Compare the purchased workflow, not the cheapest plan label. Name booking, transfer, SMS, transcript, and platform prerequisites by tier.
- Use sample call duration only to illustrate unit economics. Do not convert calls, callers, credits, interactions, and minutes into a universal ratio.
- Describe competitor source and hosting status as “not advertised on the reviewed official pages.”
- Reserve “provider and infrastructure control” for LobbyStack self-hosting. Managed LobbyStack Cloud does not give the customer the same infrastructure responsibility or control.
- Credit genuine competitor wins: Upfirst's hosted polish and language breadth, Frontdesk's omnichannel CRM bundle, Smith.ai and Moneypenny's human networks, Goodcall's unlimited-duration model, Rosie's minute allowances, and the suite integration offered by Zoom, RingCentral, Nextiva, Quo, and CloudTalk.
