## What is a phone answering service?

You forward your line to the service, for every call or only the ones you don't pick up. A person or a program answers in your company's name, then takes a message, transfers the call or books an appointment. In Quebec you'll also see the terms "centre de réponse", "téléréceptionniste" and [virtual receptionist](/blog/ai-receptionist-vs-virtual-receptionist/).

You have three formats to pick from:

- **A call centre with agents.** A person answers from the script you provide. They handle unusual calls better, and the price climbs with your volume.
- **An AI receptionist.** Software answers, books into your calendar and transfers emergencies. You pay by the minute or by the call, and a night call costs the same as a daytime one.
- **A virtual secretary.** A remote person who handles your calls and some of your admin. Our guide to [virtual secretaries in Quebec](/blog/virtual-secretary-quebec/) compares their hourly rates.

## Does Bill 96 apply to your phone line?

Yes, if you serve consumers. Since the 2022 reform known as Bill 96, article 50.2 of Quebec's [Charter of the French Language](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/fr/document/lc/C-11) requires a business that offers goods or services to consumers to respect their right to be informed and served in French. Article 5 gives consumers that right. The service that answers for you speaks in your company's name, so it needs to serve your customers in French.

Article 204.17 sets a narrow exception. A consumer can't ask a court to stop a breach of article 5 when the business had fewer than five employees at the time. The duty in article 50.2 stays the same for small businesses.

*This guide isn't legal advice. For your situation, contact the [Office québécois de la langue française](https://www.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/) or a lawyer.*

Ask any service three questions before you sign:

1. **Who answers first, and in what language?** A call centre needs French-speaking agents at every hour you buy. An AI receptionist needs to open the call in French.
2. **What language do the messages arrive in?** Your team reads the messages, but the customer may get a confirmation text.
3. **What happens with an English-speaking customer?** Can the service switch to English when the customer asks, and keep your rules?

## AI receptionist or live agents: which should you choose?

Choose live agents when your calls need judgment: an angry customer, an on-call list that changes every week, a request no script covers. A person adapts better, and you pay for that time.

## How do you choose a phone answering service in Quebec?

1. **Count your missed calls.** Check last month's call log: how many went unanswered, at what times, and how long your calls last.
2. **Decide what the service must do.** Taking a message costs less than booking an appointment or transferring an emergency. Write down the three kinds of call you get most.
3. **Ask for a written price for your volume.** Include the price per extra call or minute, the hours covered, taxes and the cancellation notice.
4. **Test the French.** Call the service yourself at 12:30 p.m. and 7:45 p.m., in French, with a hard question.
5. **Keep your number.** All of these services work through call forwarding. Our guide to [forwarding your business calls](/blog/how-to-forward-business-calls/) explains each type.
