An **AI receptionist** costs less than a human receptionist, answers at any hour and takes several calls at once. A **human receptionist** handles upset callers and unusual requests better, whether they work remotely as a virtual receptionist or at your front desk. In November 2026, AI cost about $0.19 to $0.50 a minute, live virtual receptionists about $1 to $5, and a full-time hire about $4,610 a month with benefits.

Start with the calls you get. Routine bookings and FAQs favor speed and fixed rules, while sensitive or unusual calls favor a person on the line. You can also split the work: AI takes overflow and after-hours calls, and a person takes the rest.

## AI vs human receptionist: how do they compare?

You have three ways to answer your phone: an AI receptionist, a live virtual receptionist who works remotely for an answering service, and an in-house receptionist at your front desk. AI costs less, keeps longer hours and takes more calls at once. People bring judgment and empathy, and only an employee can do work that needs someone in the building.

| Area | AI receptionist | Live virtual receptionist | In-house receptionist |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Who answers | Software built on a voice model | Trained people at an answering service | Your employee |
| Hours | Any hour the service runs | 24/7 at many services, such as Ruby and Abby Connect | One shift: a full-time hire covers 40 of the 168 hours in a week |
| Calls at once | Several on many plans; some set a cap, such as one call on ElevenLabs Reception Basic | One per agent, so callers wait during spikes | One |
| Cost (November 2026) | About $0.19 to $0.50 a minute on per-minute plans | About $1 to $5 a minute, or $10 a call at Smith.ai | About $4,610 a month with benefits, however many calls come in |
| Judgment and empathy | Polite and follows your rules, but doesn't read emotion the way a person does | Reads emotion and context, working from your script | Reads emotion and learns your business and regular customers |
| Booking | Books into a connected calendar during the call | Needs calendar access; Smith.ai charges $1.50 per booking | Books in your own systems |
| Languages | Varies by vendor: [Upfirst](https://upfirst.ai/pricing) lists 35+, [CloudTalk](https://www.cloudtalk.io/ai-receptionist/) and [ElevenLabs Reception](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/reception-ai/billing/plans-and-pricing) 70+ | Ruby, PATLive and Abby Connect answer in English and Spanish | The languages your hire speaks |
| Front-desk work | None | None | Greets visitors, signs for packages, sorts mail |
| Oversight | Recordings, transcripts and summaries of each call | Call notes and the service's quality review | Your own supervision |

The right mix depends on how many of your calls need a person. If most callers want your hours, a price or an appointment, AI covers them at the lower rate. If many calls are complaints, emergencies or long intakes, a person earns the higher price.

### In-house receptionist

The [BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/Office-and-Administrative-Support/Receptionists.htm) puts the median receptionist wage at $18.27 an hour in May 2025. Full time, that comes to about $3,170 a month (40 hours × 52 weeks ÷ 12). In the [BLS employer cost survey](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ecec.t04.htm) for June 2026, benefits made up 31.3% of what private employers spent on office and administrative support workers. If wages are the other 68.7%, a median receptionist costs about $4,610 a month. That figure is our estimate, and it leaves out recruiting, training and cover for vacations and sick days.

One hire also covers one line during one shift. Calls at lunch, after closing or during another call still go to voicemail unless you add something else.

### Per-call or per-minute billing

Per-call plans don't change with call length, and per-minute plans do. Short calls favor per-minute billing, and long intake calls favor per-call billing. For every live vendor's rate card, see [what an answering service costs](/blog/how-much-does-an-answering-service-cost/).

Two services with the same per-minute rate can also charge different amounts for the same call. SAS bills per second. [PATLive](https://www.patlive.com/faq/) bills the first minute in full, then in 6-second steps. LobbyStack counts voice usage in seconds without rounding up, and calls under 10 seconds or calls the receptionist ends as spam don't count. Ask any other service for its billing increment before you sign.

### Setup fees and other charges

Ruby, SAS, Smith.ai and Abby Connect all state that they charge no setup fee, and LobbyStack has none on any plan. Ask about per-call add-ons too, such as the $1.50 Smith.ai Virtual Front Desk adds for each booking.

## Which calls should go to AI, and which to a person?

Send AI the calls with a defined outcome, and send a person the calls that need judgment. If you get both kinds, a hybrid setup lets each one take the calls it handles best.

### Calls an AI receptionist handles well

- answering approved questions about hours, services and prices
- collecting the caller's name, callback number and reason for calling
- offering open times and booking the appointment
- texting a confirmation
- sending urgent or uncertain calls to a person

Answering more than one call at a time keeps callers off hold during storms, promotions and after-hours spikes. The system still needs an owner who keeps prices, services, schedules and transfer rules current.

### Calls that need a person

A trained person can handle emotion and ambiguity that don't fit a set workflow. Keep a person on:

- grief, crisis or emotionally charged calls
- custom professional services
- complaints that need judgment
- callers who struggle with an automated conversation
- high-value inquiries where the relationship matters
- long intake calls, on per-call plans such as Smith.ai Virtual Front Desk, which bills a 12-minute call the same as a 1-minute one

Live services have limits too. A call center serves many businesses, so its agents know less about yours than an employee does. During a spike, callers wait until an agent is free.

### What callers say about AI

In a [Gartner survey](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2024-07-09-gartner-survey-finds-64-percent-of-customers-would-prefer-that-companies-didnt-use-ai-for-customer-service) of 5,728 customers, published in July 2024, 64% said they would prefer that companies didn't use AI for customer service. Their top concern was that AI would make it harder to reach a person. If you use AI, set a transfer rule for anyone who asks for a person, and make sure someone answers it.

## Can an AI receptionist replace a human receptionist?

For routine phone work, yes. An AI receptionist can answer, book, take messages and transfer calls at any hour. It can't do the rest of a front-desk job, such as greeting walk-ins, signing for packages or calming a caller in a crisis. Keep a person reachable for the calls it hands off.

### A hybrid setup often fits best

In a hybrid setup, AI answers first and people take the calls it shouldn't handle. [Smith.ai's AI Receptionist](https://smith.ai/pricing/ai-receptionist) puts the company's own human receptionists behind its AI. Abby Connect sells human and AI answering on one plan, where an AI minute uses half an Abby Minute. With LobbyStack, the people are your own team: the receptionist transfers calls to them, or rings a staff phone before it answers.

A home-service company might let AI handle after-hours intake and booking, transfer emergencies to the on-call technician, and take messages about complaints. A law office might let AI schedule consultations while a live receptionist handles emotionally charged intake. A dental practice might use an [AI receptionist for dental offices](/solutions/ai-receptionist-for-dental-offices/) for routine booking and send clinical questions to staff. An office with a front-desk receptionist might send AI only the overflow and after-hours calls, so the person at the desk can serve the people in front of them.

Design the handoff before launch:

- **Transfer rules.** Send a caller to a person when the receptionist lacks approved information, hears urgency or frustration, or the caller asks for someone.
- **Context.** Give the person who takes over the caller's name, callback number and reason for calling, so the caller doesn't repeat everything.
- **Fallback.** Decide what happens when nobody answers a transfer, every agent is busy, or a call arrives outside the hours you paid for.

## What is a virtual receptionist?

A **virtual receptionist** answers your business calls from outside your office: a live person at an answering service, AI software, or both. It greets callers in your business name, answers questions, books appointments, takes messages and transfers urgent calls. "Virtual" means remote, so the word alone doesn't tell you whether a person or software picks up.

The job includes:

- greeting callers in your business name
- answering approved questions about hours, prices, services and directions
- screening callers, so sales pitches and spam don't reach you
- taking messages with a name, callback number and reason for calling
- booking, rescheduling or cancelling appointments, with access to your calendar
- transferring urgent calls to whoever is on duty
- sending your team an alert or a summary

### What a virtual receptionist can't do

A remote receptionist can't greet walk-ins, sign for packages or sort mail. A receptionist, human or AI, also knows only what you tell them. A live agent works from the script and notes you supply, and an AI receptionist works from its knowledge base and rules. If your prices change and nobody updates them, callers hear the old ones. A confident voice reading stale information does more harm than voicemail.

### Types of virtual receptionist

There are four types of virtual receptionist: a live person at an answering service, an AI receptionist that holds a conversation, an auto-attendant or IVR menu that routes calls, and a hybrid where AI takes routine calls and people take the rest. All four answer from outside your office.

| Type | Who or what answers | What it handles | How it bills | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Live virtual receptionist | Trained people at an answering service | Judgment calls, messages, transfers, booking with calendar access | Per minute or per call | Ruby, PATLive, Smith.ai Virtual Front Desk |
| AI receptionist | Software built on a voice model | Questions, intake, messages, booking, transfers | Per minute, per call or per unique caller | Goodcall, LobbyStack (ours), Rosie, Upfirst |
| Auto-attendant or IVR | A recorded "press 1" menu | Routing only | Often an add-on billed per minute | Specialty Answering Service's IVR |
| Hybrid | AI first, then people | Routine calls by AI, the rest by people | A mix of the two | Smith.ai AI Receptionist, Abby Connect, LobbyStack with transfers to your team |

**Live virtual receptionist.** A trained person at a company such as Ruby, PATLive or Smith.ai follows the script you provide and can use judgment when a call goes off it. Ruby and PATLive bill per minute, and Smith.ai Virtual Front Desk bills per call. Ruby answers in English and Spanish 24/7, and PATLive offers bilingual receptionists at no extra charge. Our guide to [Ruby's pricing and an AI alternative](/blog/ruby-receptionists-alternative/) lists its plans and overage rates.

**AI receptionist.** Software answers the phone and talks with callers in plain language. Some vendors call it an AI virtual receptionist. It answers from your knowledge base and books into a connected calendar. Vendors bill per minute (LobbyStack, Rosie), per call (Upfirst) or per unique caller each month (Goodcall), so compare them on your own call pattern.

**Auto-attendant or IVR.** An IVR (interactive voice response) menu plays a recording: press 1 for sales, press 2 for service. It routes calls, but it can't hold a conversation or book an appointment. SAS charges [$0.15 a minute for IVR](https://www.specialtyansweringservice.net/pricing/). ## Is a virtual receptionist worth it?

A virtual receptionist, AI or live, is worth it when the calls you miss cost more than the service does. A caller who reaches voicemail can hang up and call the next business on the list. Our [missed-call revenue calculator](/missed-call-revenue-calculator/) puts a dollar figure on those calls. To compare services, price last month's calls on each finalist's rate card and run the same booking test on each one.

### Price your own month from last month's calls

Use one month of call data:

1. Count answered and missed calls.
2. Work out the average and total call length.
3. Count the calls that needed judgment.
4. Count bookings, qualified leads, messages and transfers.
5. Add setup, integration, overage, text and staff handoff costs.

Then price that month on each finalist's own rate card. Weigh bookings above messages. A message means someone has to call back, while a booking made on the call needs nothing more.

### Run the same booking test on both

Ask each finalist to handle the same test calls:

- two service types with different lengths
- a staff member who performs only one service
- buffer time between appointments
- no availability on the requested day
- a reschedule followed by a cancellation
- a calendar outage

An AI receptionist should check live availability, apply your rules, write the appointment and confirm it. A live receptionist needs the same calendar access and training.

Ask these on sales calls and during trials, and get the answers in writing.

### Questions for a live answering service

- Who answers our calls, and where are they located?
- How many businesses does each agent cover?
- Can agents book and reschedule in our systems?
- How do you retrain agents when our policies change?
- What happens during a spike in calls?
- What's your billing increment, and do you bill hold time, after-call work, spam and very short calls?
- Is there a setup fee, and what's the overage rate?

- How does it handle interruptions and questions it can't answer?

- How do callers reach a person, and what happens if nobody answers?
- Can we control data retention and exports, or self-host the software?

## AI vs human receptionist FAQ

### Is an AI receptionist cheaper than a human receptionist?

Yes, at small-business call volumes. For 40 three-minute calls a month, the AI plans we checked in November 2026 cost $25.25 to $150, live virtual receptionists $127 to $485, and a full-time employee about $4,610 with benefits. An employee also covers front-desk work that no phone service does.

### What's the difference between an AI receptionist and a virtual receptionist?

A virtual receptionist is anyone who answers your calls remotely. Most are people at an answering service. An AI receptionist is a virtual receptionist too, with software holding the conversation instead of a person. When people compare the two, they usually mean software against a remote human.

### Is a virtual receptionist the same as a virtual assistant?

No. A virtual assistant is a remote worker who does wider admin work, such as email, scheduling and documents, and you often pay one by the hour. A virtual receptionist focuses on your phone calls: answering, booking, taking messages and transferring.

### Is a virtual receptionist legit?

Yes. Virtual receptionists are an established service category, so judge the specific company by its published prices, contract terms and trial. [SAS](https://www.specialtyansweringservice.net/pricing/) and [PATLive](https://www.patlive.com/pricing/) offer 14-day free trials, [Abby Connect](https://www.abby.com/pricing/) offers a free trial on every plan, and LobbyStack's Free plan includes a US or Canadian number for 14 days.

## Verdict

Choose a human receptionist when your calls depend on empathy, improvisation or a person on every line. A live virtual receptionist covers the phone alone, and an in-house hire also covers the front desk. Choose an AI receptionist when call volume is predictable, speed matters and the work follows clear rules.
