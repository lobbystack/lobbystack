## The best live dental answering services

All three put people on your patients' calls. Read each one's healthcare page before you sign, because that's where the HIPAA and emergency promises live.

### Ruby: best live service with a stated BAA

Ruby's [HIPAA page](https://www.ruby.com/hipaa-answering-services/) says it provides a Business Associate Agreement and doesn't charge extra for HIPAA-compliant service. Messages and call details reach you through the Ruby app and online portal instead of plain email or text. Its [dental page](https://www.ruby.com/industries/dental-virtual-receptionists/) says receptionists schedule appointments, offer 24/7 English and Spanish, and follow the escalation instructions your practice gives them. Ruby adds that its receptionists don't give clinical guidance or decide whether a patient has an emergency.

Plans differ only in minutes, from $250 for 50 to $1,725 for 500 ([pricing page](https://www.ruby.com/plans-and-pricing/)). Extra minutes cost $4.00 to $5.40, depending on the plan ([help center](https://rubyhelpcenter.helpjuice.com/billing-plans/am-i-on-the-right-billing-plan)). The dental page mentions Zapier and calendar connections but names no practice software, and tells you to confirm integrations during onboarding.

### Specialty Answering Service: best low-cost live service

Specialty Answering Service (SAS) bills by the second, from $44 a month plus $1.54 a minute, with $0 setup and a 14-day trial with no card ([pricing page](https://www.specialtyansweringservice.net/pricing/)). Its [dental page](https://www.specialtyansweringservice.net/industries/healthcare/dental-answering-service/) calls SAS a HIPAA-compliant answering service and lists "rapid dental emergency dispatch to your on-call" dentist. Agents pre-screen emergencies by your criteria and reach your staff by phone, email, text or pager.

Agents speak English and Spanish, and they book, move or confirm appointments in your online calendar or in SAS's own. The page doesn't name Dentrix, Open Dental or Eaglesoft. Patching a caller through to you costs $0.10 a minute.

### PATLive: best for new-patient intake by people

PATLive's [pricing page](https://www.patlive.com/pricing/) lists 11 tiers from $49 a month plus $2.99 a minute to 5,000 minutes, with overage falling at each step. Its [medical and wellness page](https://www.patlive.com/industries/medical-wellness/) says agents gather new patients' medical history and insurance information before the first visit. For urgent calls after hours, they relay the details to your on-call team.

Bilingual receptionists take Spanish calls from 8 a.m. to 10 p.m. Eastern at no extra charge. The page says bookings update your calendar and practice management software through native integrations, without naming a dental system. We found no HIPAA statement on that page, so ask PATLive for its BAA terms in writing.

Ask your compliance officer before you pick a vendor. This post isn't legal advice.

## How do you choose a dental answering service?

### 1. Count your missed calls

Pull a month of call logs from your phone system and count calls that rang out or reached voicemail. Split them by lunch, after close and weekends. Per-minute plans need the minutes, and flat plans only pay off above a certain volume.

### 2. Settle the compliance question first

If your practice requires a BAA, drop every vendor that won't sign one before you compare prices. Ruby and Viva state on their sites that they provide one.

### 3. Place three test calls

Call each finalist three times: a new patient asking whether you take their insurance, a patient with swelling at 9 p.m., and an existing patient moving a cleaning. Make one in Spanish if your patients speak it. Check what the summary said, where the booking landed, and whether the on-call phone rang. Trials: SAS 14 days, Dentina and Viva 30 days, PATLive free trial, and LobbyStack Free, with 30 minutes and a US or Canadian number free for 14 days.

### 4. Keep your practice number

Forward your existing number from your carrier for overflow, after-hours or every call, so patients keep dialing the number on your website. Peerlogic replaces your phone system instead. Our guide on [how to forward business calls](/blog/how-to-forward-business-calls/) covers the common carriers.
