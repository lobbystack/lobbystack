## LobbyStack costs less and gives you ownership

LobbyStack lets a business test live answering, booking, human handoff, SMS alerts, summaries, and call history without a monthly charge. Starter adds 150 minutes for $30, less than half Goodcall's $79 entry price.

Goodcall charges for each AI agent and unique caller. A multi-location company may need several subscriptions, while a stream of new callers consumes the allowance even when conversations last 30 seconds. LobbyStack meters the phone time and excludes spam and calls under 10 seconds.

Goodcall can save money when a stable customer base calls for long conversations. LobbyStack fits the common lead-capture pattern of many brief calls and gives technical teams the code, provider choice, and self-hosting route that Goodcall's reviewed pages do not advertise.

## Goodcall's unique-caller unit can be a bargain

Goodcall publicly lists:

- $79 per agent for 100 unique callers
- $129 per agent for 250 unique callers
- $249 per agent for 500 unique callers
- $0.50 for each caller above the allowance

The plans advertise unlimited minutes and tokens. If the same 80 customers call several times and hold long conversations, Goodcall can absorb that duration without consuming extra caller credits.

LobbyStack counts each voice minute. Its Free, Starter, and Pro plans include 30, 150, and 500 minutes. Repeated long calls use the allowance each time.

The result flips for a business that receives hundreds of distinct 30-second inquiries. Goodcall counts each person. LobbyStack counts the combined duration. Pull a phone log before comparing the plan labels.

## One agent can become several subscriptions

Goodcall prices per agent. A single business can configure skills, forms, logic, directory routing, transfers, call records, Zapier, and integrations around that agent.

Separate locations or distinct call experiences may need separate agents and subscriptions. The public Starter plan also lists one logic flow, three directory contacts, and seven days of call and customer history.

A small shop with one number may fit those limits. A multi-location operator should count agents, directory contacts, logic flows, and required history before estimating the bill.

LobbyStack organizes business context, phone numbers, routing, appointments, callers, messages, and call records inside the receptionist product. Enterprise supports higher-volume and multi-location needs. Self-hosted teams can extend the stack, but they also own the operation.

## Goodcall favors no-code configuration

Goodcall's skills and forms give non-technical users a way to define what the agent collects and does. Its department directory helps route callers to people or teams. That approach works for a business owner who wants to configure an agent in a browser and leave infrastructure to the vendor.

LobbyStack also lets operators configure business knowledge and call behavior through the product. Its open repository adds another route for technical teams. They can inspect the implementation, add provider integrations, or deploy the system within their own environment.

Goodcall's reviewed pages do not advertise public source code or self-hosting. LobbyStack's control requires more technical ownership.

## A property-management example

A property manager serves 140 tenants. The same tenants call about rent, maintenance, entry notices, and after-hours emergencies. Many conversations run several minutes.

Goodcall's unique-caller model may fit because repeat calls from the same tenants stay inside the caller allowance. The manager should confirm how the system identifies callers who use shared or changing numbers.

LobbyStack may fit if the manager gets many short calls from prospects, vendors, and tenants, or if it needs self-hosted control over call records. The team should test emergency transfers, address capture, duplicate contacts, and the handoff when a caller withholds their number.

This use case cannot be priced from the number of calls alone. Unique people and total minutes both matter.

## Test identity and routing before you commit

Goodcall's pricing depends on unique callers, so ask how it treats shared family numbers, blocked caller ID, forwarded calls, and a customer who changes numbers. Use examples from your phone log. A property manager may see one household call from three devices, while a medical office may receive several patients through one care facility number.

Test the routing rules with calls that cross departments. Ask the agent for leasing, report an urgent repair, then change the subject during the same call. Check whether it preserves the caller's details when it transfers the conversation.

Run the same calls through LobbyStack and compare the records your staff receives. Review the transcript, summary, transfer outcome, and any booking or follow-up message. The pricing unit matters, but your team still needs a usable record after the caller hangs up.

## Choose Goodcall when

Choose Goodcall when a stable group of customers makes long or repeated calls and you want a no-code hosted agent. Its unlimited-duration claim and unique-caller billing are meaningful advantages for that pattern.

## Choose LobbyStack when

Choose LobbyStack when you want to start free, pay $30 instead of $79 at entry, keep more than seven days of operational call records, handle many short calls, or control the code and deployment.
