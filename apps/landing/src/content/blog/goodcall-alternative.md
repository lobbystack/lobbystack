## LobbyStack costs less and gives you ownership

LobbyStack lets a business test answering, booking, summaries, and call history in the browser without a monthly charge. Goodcall offers a free demo agent for the same job, and its billing starts when you connect a number. LobbyStack Starter adds 150 minutes for $30, less than half Goodcall's $79 entry price.

Goodcall's FAQ says each location can have its own agent and tells you to choose a plan for the customers each agent serves. A multi-location company may need several subscriptions, while a stream of new callers consumes the allowance even when conversations last 30 seconds. LobbyStack meters phone time and doesn't count calls under 10 seconds or spam calls the receptionist ends.

Goodcall can save money when a stable customer base calls for long conversations. LobbyStack fits the common lead-capture pattern of many brief calls and gives technical teams the code, provider choice, and self-hosting route that Goodcall's reviewed pages do not advertise.

## Goodcall's unique-caller unit can be a bargain

Goodcall's pricing page lists:

- Starter: $79 a month for 100 unique callers (about 150 calls), then 79¢ per extra caller
- Growth: $129 a month for 500 unique callers (about 750 calls), then 26¢
- Scale: $299 a month for 2,000 unique callers (about 3,000 calls), then 15¢
- Enterprise: custom pricing above 2,000 callers a month

Every plan bills monthly, and the FAQ says Goodcall doesn't offer annual plans for now. Each plan also caps AI usage for test runs, from about 100 on Starter to about 2,000 on Scale, and Goodcall sells top-ups in $5 increments.

Goodcall counts one phone number once a month, however many times it calls, and never meters minutes. Spam and wrong numbers don't count. If the same 80 customers call several times and hold long conversations, Goodcall absorbs that duration without using extra caller credits.

LobbyStack counts each voice minute. Its Free, Starter, and Pro plans include 30, 150, and 500 minutes. Repeated long calls use the allowance each time.

The result flips for a business that receives hundreds of distinct 30-second inquiries. Goodcall counts each person. LobbyStack counts the combined duration. Pull a phone log before comparing the plan labels.

## One agent can become several subscriptions

Goodcall prices each plan by the customers one agent serves. Inside that agent, you set up workers, which Goodcall describes as specialists such as a booker, a message taker, or a knowledge expert. Starter includes 5 workers, 1 external tool, and 1 team member. Growth raises those limits to 10, 5, and 5, and Scale to 20, 15, and 10.

Separate locations or distinct call experiences may need separate agents and plans. A small shop with one number may fit Starter's limits. A multi-location operator should count agents, team members, connected tools, and required history before estimating the bill.

LobbyStack organizes business context, phone numbers, transfers, appointments, callers, messages, and call records inside the receptionist product. Enterprise covers higher call volume. Self-hosted teams can extend the stack, but they also own the operation.

## Goodcall favors no-code configuration

Goodcall's workers give non-technical users a way to define what the agent collects and does. Native connections cover Google Calendar, Google Sheets, and HighLevel, and an API tool or MCP server reaches other systems. Zapier works with older Goodcall 3 agents. Escalation contacts let the agent transfer callers to people, retry busy lines, and try up to three contacts. That approach works for a business owner who wants to configure an agent in a browser and leave infrastructure to the vendor.

LobbyStack also lets operators configure business knowledge and call behavior through the product. It books into Google Calendar and sends events to other tools through webhooks and its REST API. Its open repository adds another route for technical teams. They can inspect the implementation, add provider integrations, or deploy the system within their own environment.

Goodcall's reviewed pages do not advertise public source code or self-hosting. LobbyStack's control requires more technical ownership.

## A property-management example

A property manager serves 140 tenants. The same tenants call about rent, maintenance, entry notices, and after-hours emergencies. Many conversations run several minutes.

Goodcall's unique-caller model may fit because repeat calls from the same tenants count once a month. At 140 tenants, Starter's 100 callers plus 40 extra at 79¢ comes to about $111, and Growth covers 500 callers for $129. The manager should confirm how the system identifies callers who use shared or changing numbers.

LobbyStack may fit if the manager gets many short calls from prospects, vendors, and tenants, or if it needs self-hosted control over call records. The team should test emergency transfers, address capture, duplicate contacts, and the handoff when a caller withholds their number.

This use case cannot be priced from the number of calls alone. Unique people and total minutes both matter.

## Test identity and routing before you commit

Goodcall's pricing depends on unique callers, so ask how it treats shared family numbers, blocked caller ID, forwarded calls, and a customer who changes numbers. Use examples from your phone log. A property manager may see one household call from three devices, while a medical office may receive several patients through one care facility number.

Test the routing rules with calls that cross departments. Ask the agent for leasing, report an urgent repair, then change the subject during the same call. Check whether it preserves the caller's details when it transfers the conversation, and what happens when the first contact doesn't answer.

Run the same calls through LobbyStack and compare the records your staff receives. LobbyStack hands the call to one transfer number without briefing the person who answers. If the transfer can't go through, the receptionist offers to take a message, and your team can get a "Live call transfer failed" alert. Review the transcript, summary, transfer outcome, and any booking or message.

## Choose Goodcall when

Choose Goodcall when a stable group of customers makes long or repeated calls and you want a no-code hosted agent. Unmetered minutes, unique-caller billing, and transfers that try up to three contacts are meaningful advantages for that pattern. Scale also keeps call history with no time limit.

## Choose LobbyStack when

Choose LobbyStack when you want to start free, pay $30 instead of $79 at entry, keep 90 days of recordings and transcripts on a $30 plan, handle many short calls, or control the code and deployment.
