## What is a Ruby receptionist?

Ruby (ruby.com), from Ruby Receptionists, Inc., is a live virtual receptionist service. Its US-based receptionists answer your business calls 24/7, including weekends and holidays, and you decide when Ruby steps in. Ruby's FAQ says it serves businesses anywhere in the United States, Canada and the US Caribbean.

Ruby uses AI to support its staff and leaves the calls to its receptionists. Its FAQ says an AI receptionist that picks up and runs the call on its own "isn't how Ruby works."

Every Ruby receptionist plan lists the same services, so the plans differ in minutes alone. Ruby's pricing and answering-service pages list:

- answering, transfers, and messages sent to you by email, text or app notification
- appointment scheduling, lead intake and payment collection
- outbound call assists, such as confirming an appointment
- Spanish-speaking receptionists at no extra cost
- local or toll-free number hosting, and calls and texts from your business number
- voicemail transcription, AI transcripts and caller sentiment analysis
- robocall filtering
- HIPAA-compliant services
- "5000+ integrations", through direct integrations and Zapier

### How does Ruby count a receptionist minute?

Ruby's [terms of use](https://www.ruby.com/terms/), last updated March 2026, set four rules:

- **Rounding.** Ruby bills in 60-second increments and rounds each call up to the next 60-second mark, so a 61-second call uses 2 minutes.
- **Hold time counts.** Talk time after a receptionist transfers the call to you or your voicemail doesn't.
- **After-call work counts.** The clock includes the time a receptionist spends completing your call notes after the caller hangs up.
- **Outbound calls count.** On an outbound call assist, Ruby bills the receptionist's time on the call and the follow-up email.

Ruby's FAQ uses a 10-second call as its example and says Ruby bills it as 60 seconds "(or half of a receptionist minute)." That parenthesis conflicts with the 60-second rule, so ask Ruby which one applies.

Ruby routes robocalls to your Ruby voicemail box. Its [answering-service page](https://www.ruby.com/answering-service/) says "we don't answer them live or charge you for them." The pages we read don't say how Ruby bills a wrong number or a spam call that reaches a receptionist. Ruby alerts you when you've used 85% and 100% of your plan's minutes.

### What are Ruby's contract terms?

- **Term.** Your contract renews every 30 days. A cancellation takes effect at the end of the current monthly billing period.
- **Upgrades.** An upgrade starts at once and applies to the current billing period "and at least the subsequent two consecutive billing periods."
- **Downgrades.** You can downgrade after three consecutive billing periods on a plan. New customers get a 90-day calibration window to change plans at any time. Returning customers don't.
- **Money-back guarantee.** First-time users get a full refund if they cancel within 21 days of buying or before they pass 500 receptionist minutes, whichever comes first. Ruby doesn't refund other payments.
- **Setup fees.** The pricing page says Ruby has "no additional or hidden fees for activation, onboarding, setup, customization, or coverage during certain periods." The terms mention setup or activation fees "as applicable" and name no amount.

The upgrade rule matters after a busy month. Say a Call Ruby 100 customer uses 200 minutes in June and then goes back to 100. Paying overage costs $845 for June ($395 + 100 × $4.50) and $1,635 across June, July and August. Upgrading to Call Ruby 200 costs $720 for June, but the terms keep the customer there through August: $2,160 for the three months.

## Questions about Ruby receptionist pricing

### Does Ruby offer a free trial?

The Ruby pages we read list no free trial. First-time users get a money-back guarantee instead: cancel within 21 days of buying, or before you pass 500 receptionist minutes, whichever comes first, and Ruby refunds the cancelled service. LobbyStack's Free plan doesn't expire. It gives you 30 voice minutes a month without a card, and a US or Canadian number free for 14 days.

### Does Ruby charge for spam calls?

Ruby sends robocalls its filter catches to your Ruby voicemail box and says it doesn't answer or charge for them. Its pages don't say how it bills a wrong number or spam that reaches a receptionist, and its terms bill any answered call as at least one minute. LobbyStack doesn't count calls under 10 seconds or calls its receptionist ends as spam.

### Is Ruby cheaper than hiring a receptionist?

At low call volumes, yes. The [Bureau of Labor Statistics](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/office-and-administrative-support/receptionists.htm) puts the median receptionist wage at $18.27 an hour, or $38,010 a year, as of May 2025, before payroll taxes and benefits. Ruby's 200-minute plan costs $8,640 a year. A hire covers one shift, while Ruby covers 24/7 for the minutes you buy.

### What is the cheapest AI answering service?

The lowest paid entry we found in October 2026 was [AI-Receptionist.com](https://ai-receptionist.com/pricing) at $14 a month for 75 minutes. [Smith.ai's AI Receptionist](https://smith.ai/pricing/ai-receptionist) has a $0 plan for 25 calls a month, then $3 a call. ### Does Ruby use AI to answer calls?

No. Ruby's receptionists answer, and its AI works behind the scenes. A tool called Call Nav pulls up your business details during the call, and AI writes call and voicemail transcripts, scores caller sentiment and filters robocalls. Ruby's AI page says these features come at no extra cost on every plan.

### Can I keep my business number if I leave Ruby?

If your carrier holds the number and forwards calls to Ruby, change the forwarding rule to point at LobbyStack. If Ruby hosts the number, ask Ruby how to transfer it out before you cancel, and contact the LobbyStack team to port it. LobbyStack offers numbers in the US, Canada, the UK, Australia and New Zealand.
