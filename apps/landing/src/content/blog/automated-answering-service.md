An **automated answering service** picks up business calls when your team can't. The three types are a phone tree (IVR) that routes callers by keypad, voicemail that turns messages into text, and an AI receptionist that talks with callers, books appointments and transfers urgent calls. AI receptionist entry plans run $14 to $150 a month (October 2026).

## What is an automated answering service?

An automated answering service is software that answers calls on your business line without a person. Depending on the type, it routes the caller, records a message, or talks with the caller to answer questions and book appointments. You can keep your current number by forwarding it to the service.

### Other names for an automated answering service

- **Auto attendant** or **IVR** (interactive voice response): a recorded menu where callers press a key or say a word to be routed.
- **Automated receptionist**, **automated phone answering system** or **automated phone answering service**: loose labels that can mean any type in this guide, so ask which one a vendor sells.
- **AI receptionist** or **AI answering service**: software that talks with callers, answers from your business information, and can book or transfer.
- **Virtual receptionist**: often means a remote human receptionist. See [what a virtual receptionist is](/blog/ai-receptionist-vs-virtual-receptionist/) and how one compares with an AI receptionist.

### How calls reach it

You either get a new number from the provider or forward your existing line to it. Forwarding lets customers keep dialing the number on your website. You choose which calls the service gets with your carrier's forwarding rule: unanswered calls, after-hours calls or every call. Step 6 below covers the choice.

This guide covers business phone lines. The auto-answer setting on an iPhone or Android phone is a different feature.

## What are the types of automated answering services?

Automated answering services come in three types. A phone tree (IVR or auto attendant) routes callers through a menu, voicemail-to-text records and transcribes messages, and an AI receptionist holds a conversation and can book or transfer. A live answering service, staffed by remote human receptionists, is the non-automated alternative to all three.

| Type | What the caller does | Can it book? | How vendors bill | Fits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Phone tree (IVR or auto attendant) | Presses a key or says a word | No | Per-minute add-on or part of a phone plan | Routing between departments |
| Voicemail-to-text | Leaves a message | No | Per-minute add-on or part of a phone plan | Low volume, calls that can wait |
| AI receptionist | Talks and asks questions | On most plans | Per minute, per call or per unique caller | Booking and after-hours calls |
| Live answering service (human, for comparison) | Talks to a person | On many plans; [Smith.ai Virtual Front Desk](https://smith.ai/pricing/receptionists) charges $1.50 per call for booking | Per minute or per call | Calls that need judgment |

### Phone tree (IVR or auto attendant)

A phone tree plays a menu ("press 1 for service, press 2 for billing") and connects callers to the line they pick. Some menus accept a spoken word instead of a key press. Phone systems often call this feature an auto attendant. It's cheap and predictable when callers know who they want. It can't answer an open question, take booking details or spot an urgent call.

LobbyStack doesn't sell a keypad menu. Callers speak, and the receptionist answers them or transfers the call under your rules.

### Voicemail-to-text

Voicemail-to-text records a message and turns it into a transcript. The caller explains the problem, leaves a number and waits for you to call back. It suits low call volume and callers who don't mind waiting, such as vendors and existing clients. [AI receptionist vs voicemail](/blog/ai-receptionist-vs-voicemail/) covers where voicemail still works and where it falls short.

### AI receptionist

An AI receptionist talks with callers in everyday speech. It answers questions from your business information, books into your calendar, transfers urgent calls and takes messages. You set its behavior in plain language instead of building a menu. LobbyStack's version is on the [AI phone answering](/solutions/ai-phone-answering/) page.

### Live answering service, the human option

A live answering service puts remote human receptionists on your calls. They handle calls that need judgment, such as an upset customer or a legal intake, and bill per minute or per call at higher rates than software. The cost section below compares the bills.

## Can I use AI to answer my phone calls?

Yes. An AI receptionist picks up your business line, follows the rules you write, answers questions from your business information, books appointments, transfers urgent calls and takes messages. You decide which calls it gets through your carrier's forwarding, and which calls go to a person through its transfer settings.

### What an AI receptionist does on a call

With LobbyStack as the example:

- You write the greeting, tone and call rules in plain language, and it follows the rules in your priority order.
- It answers from your knowledge base: website pages, PDF, Word (.docx), text and Markdown files, and FAQs you write.
- Depending on the booking mode you pick, it books into Google Calendar, takes a request for your team to confirm, or takes a message. Booking needs your opening hours.

- Your team gets an email alert for each message, and can turn on SMS alerts to a US or Canadian mobile.
- Each call gets a recording, a transcript and a one-line summary.

## How do I set up an automated answering service?

Set one up in seven steps: list your calls, pick the type that fits them, write the greeting, load your business facts, write call rules, choose which calls it answers through carrier forwarding, and test it before real callers reach it. The steps work for any type, with LobbyStack as the example.

### 1. List your calls and the ones a person must take

Pull two to four weeks of calls from your call log and sort them: bookings, price and hours questions, existing customers, vendors and spam. Then mark the calls a person must take, such as emergencies and complaints. Use that list to pick the type and write your transfer rules.

### 2. Pick the type that matches those calls

- Routing between people or departments: a phone tree.
- Messages that can wait a day: voicemail-to-text.
- Booking requests and repeat questions: an AI receptionist.
- Calls that need judgment or a human voice: a live answering service.

You can combine two, such as an AI receptionist that transfers emergencies to a person.

### 3. Write the greeting callers hear first

Give the business name and say what the caller can do, in under 25 words:

> "Thanks for calling [business name]. I can book a visit, answer questions about our services, or take a message. How can I help?"

If the service records calls, as LobbyStack does, add a recording notice where local law requires one. For a phone tree, offer only the options most callers need.

### 4. Give it your business facts

Collect your hours, services, prices or price ranges, service area and the questions customers ask most. An AI receptionist's answers about your business are only as complete as what you give it. LobbyStack imports your website pages and accepts PDF, Word (.docx), text and Markdown files plus FAQs you type, up to 5 MB of text on Starter and 20 MB on Pro.

### 5. Write call rules in plain language

Write what should happen on each call type from step 1, most important first: urgent calls, then transfers, booking and messages. One rule might read: "If a caller says they have no heat, treat it as urgent and transfer the call." LobbyStack follows your rules in the order you give them. For more examples, see [call rules in plain language](/blog/ai-receptionist-workflows/).

### 6. Choose which calls it answers

You set the coverage with your carrier's call forwarding:

| Forwarding rule at your carrier | What you get |
| --- | --- |
| Forward when busy or unanswered | Overflow: the service takes calls your team misses |
| Forward at closing time | After-hours coverage |
| Forward every call | Full-time answering |

LobbyStack answers whatever calls reach its number. Forward your number to keep it, or contact the team to port it. LobbyStack numbers are available in the US, Canada, the UK and Australia. If nights and weekends are your gap, see [after-hours answering](/solutions/after-hours-answering-service/).

### 7. Test it before you forward real calls

Call it yourself before customers do. LobbyStack's Free plan gives you 30 browser voice minutes a month for testing, with no card and no telephone number. Script an emergency, a booking and a caller who speaks Spanish or another language your customers use. After you forward your line, read the first week's recordings, transcripts and summaries, and fix any rule that misfires.

## Which automated answering service fits your business?

Pick by the calls you get most. A phone tree fits routing between departments, voicemail-to-text fits calls that can wait, an AI receptionist fits booking and after-hours calls, and a live service fits calls that need judgment. You can also pair an AI receptionist with transfers to your staff for urgent calls.

### Choose a phone tree if callers need routing

A phone tree fits a business with several departments or staff, where callers know who they want. [PATLive](https://www.patlive.com/pricing/) includes a phone system with auto attendants in its live plans, so a menu can send some callers to your staff while a receptionist takes the rest.

### Choose voicemail-to-text if calls can wait

Voicemail fits low call volume and callers who will wait, such as vendors and existing clients. If most calls come from new customers, they wait for your callback, and some may call another business instead.

### Choose an AI receptionist if callers want to book

AI receptionists fit service businesses that run on bookings and need after-hours coverage. Other vendors win on specific points:

- [AI-Receptionist.com](https://ai-receptionist.com/pricing) was the cheapest at 40 calls in our check, though SMS starts at its $39 tier.
- [Upfirst](https://upfirst.ai/pricing) connects Outlook, Calendly and Acuity, and doesn't bill spam, calls under 15 seconds or silent callers.
- [Smith.ai](https://smith.ai/pricing/ai-receptionist) has a free AI plan that answers 25 real calls a month.
- [Goodcall](https://www.goodcall.com/pricing) bills per unique caller, so repeat callers cost nothing extra.
- [RingCentral AIR](https://www.ringcentral.com/pricing/ai-receptionist.html) works with any phone system and connects to Salesforce, HubSpot and Zoho.

LobbyStack (ours) fits owners who want booking, transfers and confirmation texts on a $30 plan with 150 minutes. It also suits businesses whose callers speak languages other than English and Spanish, and it offers a free browser test and MIT-licensed code you can self-host. Pick another vendor if you need a calendar other than Google, a practice-management sync, AI replies to texts or a keypad menu.

### Choose a live service if calls need judgment

Live answering fits legal intake, sensitive calls and callers who want a person. [Ruby](https://www.ruby.com/plans-and-pricing/) and [PATLive](https://www.patlive.com/pricing/) include Spanish-speaking receptionists at no extra cost. In our check, 40 calls cost $127 to $720 with a live service, against $25.25 to $99 on AI plans.
