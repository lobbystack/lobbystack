## What does a law firm answering service need to handle?

Six needs set legal answering apart from answering for a plumber or a salon. A service can handle general calls well and still miss on any of these, so test each one before you sign.

- **New-client intake:** the caller's name and contact details, the type of matter, key dates and how they found you, asked in the order your intake form uses.
- **Urgent matters:** an arrest, a hearing tomorrow or a protective order. These calls need to reach an attorney now, by transfer or by an alert to call back.
- **Confidentiality:** callers share sensitive facts on the first call, and the service writes them down or records them. You need to know who can see that record.
- **Conflict checks:** the service can collect the names of the parties. Your firm decides whether a conflict exists, and only one service in this guide sells a check against your CRM.
- **Callers in other languages:** most legal services here answer in English and Spanish, and some AI receptionists cover dozens of languages.
- **After hours:** nights, weekends and holidays, when nobody at the firm is there to pick up.

## Who runs the conflict check, and how do you protect confidentiality?

Your firm runs the conflict check. An answering service collects the names you need, and in this guide only Smith.ai's live receptionists sell a check against your CRM, at $0.50 a call. The same goes for confidentiality. Whoever answers, your firm decides what the service collects and who can see it.

Two ABA Model Rules bear on that first call. Rule 1.18 protects what a prospective client tells a lawyer even if the firm never takes the case. Rule 5.3 requires lawyers who supervise nonlawyer help, outside services included, to make reasonable efforts to keep that help in line with the lawyer's own duties. Each state adopts its own version, so ask your bar how yours applies.

In practice, that points to a short first call:

1. Collect the caller's name, contact details, the type of matter and the names of the other parties.
2. Hold the detailed story until your firm has run its conflict check.
3. Have the receptionist say it can't give legal advice and that an attorney will follow up.
4. If the service records calls, add a recording notice to the greeting where your state requires one.

Then ask each finalist, in writing, who can see your call records and how long it keeps them. Smith.ai's legal page says it doesn't share your lead or client information with other customers, and Abby Connect and Veza say they train receptionists on confidentiality. LobbyStack saves a summary, transcript and recording of every call in your dashboard, and it makes no compliance certification claim. Its code is MIT-licensed, so you can self-host it and keep stored call records on your own servers. The phone carrier and OpenAI's voice model still process the audio while a call is live.

## How do you choose a law firm answering service?

### 1. Count calls and minutes

Pull last month's call count and average call length from your phone bill. Per-call plans need the count, and per-minute plans need the minutes. Price a quiet month and a busy one, since a plan sized for your busiest month leaves minutes unused in the quiet ones.

### 2. Place three test calls during the trial

Call each finalist as a new personal-injury lead at 9 p.m., as an existing client whose hearing is tomorrow, and as a Spanish-speaking caller. Check who answered, what the intake captured, and whether the urgent call reached an attorney. Trials: Veza and PATLive run 14 days, Answering Legal gives 400 free minutes, Upfirst runs 14 days with no card, Smith.ai's AI Free plan covers 25 calls a month, and LobbyStack Free gives you 30 voice minutes and a US or Canadian number free for 14 days.

### 3. Read the billing terms

A 20-second wrong number bills as a full minute at Ruby and PATLive and as 30 seconds at Abby Connect. LobbyStack counts it as 20 seconds. Upfirst skips calls under 15 seconds, and Smith.ai's pricing pages don't say how they count short calls. Then check setup fees, retainer fees and notice periods: Smith.ai's AI plans need 30 days' notice to cancel.

### 4. Test the integration and get confidentiality terms in writing

Have the service create a test lead in Clio, MyCase or Lawmatics, and confirm which fields arrive. Ask who can see recordings and transcripts, and how long the service keeps them.
