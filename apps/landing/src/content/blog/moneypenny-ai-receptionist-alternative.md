## LobbyStack preserves budget when you already have people

Consider 100 calls that average three minutes. Moneypenny's published 100-call AI plan costs $199. LobbyStack Pro covers the resulting 300 minutes inside its $100 allowance and leaves another 200 minutes available.

The comparison changes if your company needs Moneypenny's receptionists to take escalations. Moneypenny sells live answering by the minute, from $165 for 50 minutes, so ask how escalated calls from an AI plan are billed before you budget. A business with an owner, dispatcher, or on-call employee already available does not need to pay for an external receptionist team.

LobbyStack transfers the call to that employee's number and keeps the transcript and summary in the dashboard. The receptionist doesn't brief the person who answers. The company saves on software, controls when transfers happen, and can use managed cloud or its own infrastructure.

## Moneypenny sells operational support

Moneypenny built its name around human answering. Its AI receptionist adds lead qualification, appointment scheduling, call routing, FAQ handling, data collection, voice cloning, caller analytics, real-time transcripts, and call summaries by email or text. Moneypenny says calls can escalate to one of its receptionists, available 24/7, or to a member of the customer's team.

That model can help a business without employees available for transfers. Moneypenny sells AI answering and live receptionists under one brand, on separate price lists.

LobbyStack transfers calls to one number the business sets, and it does not include an external receptionist workforce. A company can make another answering service's number its transfer number, though it owns that relationship. If a transfer can't go through, the receptionist offers to take a message, and the team can get a "Live call transfer failed" alert.

Moneypenny's live People Answering plans bill by the minute: $165 for 50 minutes, $265 for 100, $422 for 200, and $985 for 500. Extra minutes cost $2.28 on the smallest plan and $1.67 on the largest.

LobbyStack lists 150 voice minutes for $30 and 500 for $100. The unit differs from Moneypenny's AI plans, and LobbyStack has no receptionists of its own.

A five-minute call consumes one Moneypenny call allowance and five LobbyStack minutes. Ten 30-second calls consume ten Moneypenny call allowances and five LobbyStack minutes. ## Managed onboarding or inspectable deployment

Moneypenny emphasizes managed onboarding. A Solutions Architect maps your call flows and builds the knowledge base, and Moneypenny says going live takes days. That can reduce setup risk for an owner who wants someone to shape the receptionist and remain available when calls change.

LobbyStack's cloud is self-serve, with no setup fee on any plan and Enterprise support for more complex needs. The application code is public, and a technical team can self-host. It can choose providers, configure retention, inspect call behavior, and adapt the product.

The Moneypenny pages reviewed for this article do not advertise self-hosting or public source access. The absence of those options may not matter to a small business. It matters to agencies, regulated teams, and buyers who require an exit path from a hosted platform.

## A funeral-home example

A funeral home receives routine service questions and calls from people in distress. It wants 24-hour coverage, but the staff cannot take every escalation overnight.

Moneypenny can be the stronger fit if trained external receptionists can take sensitive calls under clear instructions. The business should ask how escalations from the AI plan are billed, compare that with a People Answering plan, and review agent training, scripts, and escalation timing.

LobbyStack can fit if the AI should collect only approved information and transfer every sensitive call to an on-call employee. Self-hosting may help the operator set stricter data controls, but it does not supply the missing person.

The best product is the one that respects the emotional and operational demands of the call.

## Ask who handles the exception

Map the calls that the AI should not finish. Include a distressed caller, a request outside policy, an emergency transfer that nobody answers, and a caller who refuses to share details. Ask Moneypenny whether its AI passes those calls to its receptionists, your staff, or a message queue. Confirm the hours and fees for each route.

Configure the same boundaries in LobbyStack. Your employee needs a clear on-call schedule. LobbyStack doesn't brief the person who picks up a transfer, so expect the caller to explain the situation again. Test the transcript, summary, caller consent, and the fallback when the transfer can't go through: the receptionist offers to take a message, and your team can get an alert.

Moneypenny can supply human capacity that your company lacks, at live-answering prices. LobbyStack gives your team control over the automation and transfers, but your team supplies the human response. Price that staffing before choosing.
