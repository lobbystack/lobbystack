## LobbyStack gives you more room to prove the workflow

Zoom's standalone plan includes 100 minutes. LobbyStack Starter includes 150 for one cent more. You can test 30 browser voice minutes on Free, without a telephone number. On annual billing, LobbyStack costs $1 less per month and includes 50 more minutes.

That extra capacity gives a small business more room to test booking rules, after-hours transfers, caller questions, and failure handling. The team can review transcripts and summaries before forwarding all traffic.

Zoom brings a familiar communications brand, 23 supported languages, and warm transfers. LobbyStack brings more included minutes and ownership of the stack. A technical buyer can keep the managed service or move to infrastructure and providers it controls.

## Zoom reduced the phone-system tradeoffs

Zoom says the receptionist works with any phone system, with no Zoom Phone required. Buyers who only want AI coverage avoid a forced migration to Zoom Phone. Zoom Phone customers can add the receptionist instead, starting at $0.25 a minute billed annually, on top of the Zoom Phone price.

The official pages advertise 24/7 answering, knowledge from web pages and FAQs, booking, warm transfers, transcripts, real-time analytics, and calendar integrations with Zoom Scheduler, Google, Microsoft, and Calendly. The product page also lists SMS reminders and links, while the pricing table puts SMS under the separate Virtual Agent plan, so confirm which texts your plan sends.

This combination gives a small business a low-priced service from a large communications vendor. Teams already using Zoom administration may value consolidated identity, billing, support, and security review.

LobbyStack works as a focused receptionist product. Businesses can use its managed service or connect a self-hosted deployment to their providers and infrastructure.

## Ownership creates the larger difference

Zoom provides a managed product inside its proprietary platform. Its reviewed pages do not advertise public source access or self-hosted deployment.

LobbyStack publishes the receptionist code under the MIT License. A business can inspect the call logic, run the managed cloud, or deploy the stack itself. Self-hosting lets the operator choose provider accounts, set retention, schedule upgrades, and adapt integrations.

That freedom requires someone to run the system. Zoom's managed service is a better fit when the business wants one vendor to own operation and support.

## A multi-office professional-services example

An accounting firm has four offices and an existing phone provider. It wants one receptionist to answer common questions, identify the office, schedule consultations, and transfer current clients.

Zoom may fit if it connects to the existing phone system without changing extensions and if the firm's Microsoft or Google calendar configuration matches the advertised booking flow. The IT team should test transfers by name, office hours, permissions, and reporting across locations.

LobbyStack transfers calls to one number per business and does not route callers across several offices. It may fit if each office runs its own receptionist, or if the firm needs a deployment inside controlled infrastructure. The team should test calendar access, failover, and the work required to operate the stack.

Both products should face the same calls before the firm forwards a public number.

## Check the Zoom account requirements

Confirm whether the standalone price applies to your region, contract term, and expected call volume. Ask about extra minutes, number, calling, and implementation charges. The standalone product doesn't require Zoom Phone, but Zoom's FAQ ties appointment scheduling to a Zoom Scheduler license, so ask whether your calendar setup needs one.

Then test a transfer between the AI receptionist and an existing Zoom queue. Have one caller request a named employee and another call after hours with an urgent issue. Check the caller ID, context, and fallback when nobody answers.

Run those calls through LobbyStack with your chosen phone provider. LobbyStack hands the call to one transfer number without briefing the person who answers. If the transfer can't go through, the receptionist offers to take a message, and your team can get a "Live call transfer failed" alert. A clean Zoom handoff may outweigh source access for a company that already runs its phones there. A team with provider contracts or infrastructure rules should calculate the work of fitting Zoom into those constraints.
