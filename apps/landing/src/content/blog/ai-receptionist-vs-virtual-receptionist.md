A **virtual receptionist** answers your business calls from outside your office: a live person at an answering service, AI software, or both. It greets callers in your business name, answers questions, books appointments, takes messages and transfers urgent calls. In October 2026, live services charged about $1 to $5 a minute, and per-minute AI plans about $0.19 to $0.50.

*Prices checked October 9, 2026. LobbyStack wrote this guide and sells an AI receptionist, so we include our own plan below and label it as ours.*

If you're weighing an AI vs virtual receptionist, start with the calls you get. Routine bookings and FAQs favor speed and fixed rules, while sensitive or unusual calls favor a person on the line.

## What does a virtual receptionist do?

A virtual receptionist handles the phone side of a front-desk job from somewhere else. It picks up in your business name, deals with routine calls itself and passes the rest to your team as a message or a live transfer. This guide is for businesses that want to hire one.

The job includes:

- greeting callers in your business name
- answering approved questions about hours, prices, services and directions
- screening callers, so sales pitches and spam don't reach you
- taking messages with a name, callback number and reason for calling
- booking, rescheduling or cancelling appointments, with access to your calendar
- transferring urgent calls to whoever is on duty
- sending your team an alert or a summary

### What a virtual receptionist can't do

A remote receptionist can't greet walk-ins, sign for packages or sort mail. A receptionist, human or AI, also knows only what you tell them. A live agent works from the script and notes you supply, and an AI receptionist works from its knowledge base and rules. If your prices change and nobody updates them, callers hear the old ones. A confident voice reading stale information does more harm than voicemail.

## What are the types of virtual receptionist?

There are four types of virtual receptionist: a live person at an answering service, an AI receptionist that holds a conversation, an auto-attendant or IVR menu that routes calls, and a hybrid where AI takes routine calls and people take the rest. A receptionist can be remote. All four types answer from outside your office, which is what "virtual" means.

| Type | Who or what answers | What it handles | How it bills | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Live virtual receptionist | Trained people at an answering service | Judgment calls, messages, transfers, booking with calendar access | Per minute or per call | Ruby, PATLive, Smith.ai Virtual Front Desk |
| AI receptionist | Software built on a voice model | Questions, intake, messages, booking, transfers | Per minute, per call or per unique caller | Goodcall, LobbyStack (ours), Rosie, Upfirst |
| Auto-attendant or IVR | A recorded "press 1" menu | Routing only | Free with some plans, or per minute | PATLive's free auto attendant, Specialty Answering Service IVR |
| Hybrid | AI first, then people | Routine calls by AI, the rest by people | A mix of the two | Smith.ai AI Receptionist, Abby Connect, LobbyStack with transfers to your team |

### Live virtual receptionist (answering service)

A live virtual receptionist is a trained person at a company such as [Ruby](https://www.ruby.com/plans-and-pricing/), [PATLive](https://www.patlive.com/pricing/) or [Smith.ai](https://smith.ai/pricing/receptionists). The agent follows the script you provide and can use judgment when a call goes off it. Ruby and PATLive bill per minute, and Smith.ai Virtual Front Desk bills per call. Ruby and [Abby Connect](https://www.abby.com/pricing/) include Spanish 24/7, and PATLive offers Spanish-speaking receptionists, all at no extra cost.

### AI receptionist

An AI receptionist is software that answers the phone and talks with callers in plain language. Some vendors call it an AI virtual receptionist. It answers from your knowledge base and books into a connected calendar. Many plans take more than one call at a time, though some set a cap: [ElevenLabs Reception's](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/reception-ai/billing/plans-and-pricing) Basic plan takes one. Vendors bill per minute (LobbyStack, Rosie), per call (Upfirst) or per unique caller each month (Goodcall), so compare them on your own call pattern.

### Auto-attendant or IVR phone menu

An auto-attendant, or IVR (interactive voice response), plays a recorded menu: press 1 for sales, press 2 for service. It routes calls, but it can't hold a conversation or book an appointment. PATLive includes a free auto attendant, and Specialty Answering Service (SAS) charges [$0.15 a minute for IVR](https://www.specialtyansweringservice.net/pricing/). Our [automated answering service](/blog/automated-answering-service/) guide compares phone menus with AI answering.

### Hybrid: AI first, people for the rest

In a hybrid setup, AI answers first and people take the calls it shouldn't handle. [Smith.ai's AI Receptionist](https://smith.ai/pricing/ai-receptionist) offers live backup from its human receptionists. Abby Connect puts human and AI answering on one plan, where an AI minute uses half an Abby Minute. With LobbyStack, the receptionist transfers calls it shouldn't handle to your own team.

### How each service counts a minute

Two services with the same per-minute rate can charge different amounts for the same call. SAS bills per second. PATLive bills the first minute in full, then in 6-second steps. Abby Connect bills in 30-second steps, Ruby rounds each call up to the next 60-second mark, and Smith.ai Virtual Front Desk bills per call. The [answering service cost guide](/blog/how-much-does-an-answering-service-cost/) lists the increment for every live vendor we checked. LobbyStack counts voice usage in seconds without rounding up, and calls under 10 seconds or calls the receptionist ends as spam don't count.

Many live services state no setup fee, including Ruby, SAS, Smith.ai and Abby Connect. [Davinci Virtual](https://www.davincivirtual.com/live-receptionists) charges a one-time $95 setup fee for its live receptionist service and [Ambs Call Center](https://www.ambscallcenter.com/answering-service-pricing) charges $85, so the range we found is $0 to $95. None of the AI vendors we checked publishes a setup fee, and LobbyStack has no setup fee on any plan. Also ask about per-call add-ons, such as the $1.50 Smith.ai Virtual Front Desk adds for each booking, and about holiday fees.

### Where an AI receptionist works well

AI fits calls with a defined outcome:

- answering approved questions about hours, services and prices
- collecting the caller's name, callback number and reason for calling
- offering open times and booking the appointment
- texting a confirmation
- sending urgent or uncertain calls to a person

Answering more than one call at a time keeps callers off hold during storms, promotions and after-hours spikes. The system still needs an owner who keeps prices, services, schedules and transfer rules current.

### Where a live receptionist earns the cost

A trained person can handle emotion and ambiguity that don't fit a set workflow. Choose live coverage for:

- grief, crisis or emotionally charged calls
- custom professional services
- complaints that need judgment
- callers who struggle with an automated conversation
- high-value inquiries where the relationship matters
- long intake calls, on per-call plans such as Smith.ai Virtual Front Desk, which bills a 12-minute call the same as a 1-minute one

Live services have limits too. A call center serves many businesses, so its agents know less about yours than an employee does. During a spike, callers wait until an agent is free.

## Is a virtual receptionist worth it?

A virtual receptionist is worth it when the calls you miss cost more than the service does. A caller who reaches voicemail can hang up and call the next business on the list. To check, price last month's calls on each finalist's rate card and run the same booking test on each one.

### Price your own month from last month's calls

Use one month of call data:

1. Count answered and missed calls.
2. Work out the average and total call length.
3. Count the calls that needed judgment.
4. Count bookings, qualified leads, messages and transfers.
5. Add setup, integration, overage, text and staff handoff costs.

Then price that month on each finalist's own rate card. Short calls favor per-minute billing, and long calls favor per-call billing. Weigh bookings above messages. A message means someone has to call back, while a booking made on the call needs nothing more.

### Run the same booking test on both

Ask each finalist to handle the same test calls:

- two service types with different lengths
- a staff member who performs only one service
- buffer time between appointments
- no availability on the requested day
- a reschedule followed by a cancellation
- a calendar outage

An AI receptionist should check live availability, apply your rules, write the appointment and confirm it. A live receptionist needs the same calendar access and training.

### A hybrid setup often fits best

A home-service company might let AI handle after-hours intake and booking, transfer emergencies to the on-call technician, and take messages about complaints. A law office might let AI schedule consultations while a live receptionist handles emotionally charged intake. A dental practice might use an [AI receptionist for dental offices](/solutions/ai-receptionist-for-dental-offices/) for routine booking and send clinical questions to staff.

Design the handoff before launch:

- **Transfer rules.** Send a caller to a person when the receptionist lacks approved information, hears urgency or frustration, or the caller asks for someone.
- **Context.** Give the person who takes over the caller's name, callback number and reason for calling, so the caller doesn't repeat everything.
- **Fallback.** Decide what happens when nobody answers a transfer, every agent is busy, or a call arrives outside the hours you paid for.

## What to ask before you choose a virtual receptionist

Ask these on sales calls and during trials, and get the answers in writing.

### Questions for a live answering service

- Who answers our calls, and where are they located?
- How many businesses does each agent cover?
- Can agents book and reschedule in our systems?
- How do you retrain agents when our policies change?
- What happens during a spike in calls?
- What's your billing increment, and do you bill hold time, after-call work, spam and very short calls?
- Is there a setup fee, and what's the overage rate? Ruby, for one, doesn't publish its overage rate.

For named picks, see our list of the [best answering services for small business](/blog/best-answering-service-for-small-business/).

### Questions for an AI receptionist

- Can we review recordings, transcripts and failed calls?
- Which calendar actions can it complete during a call?
- How does it handle interruptions and questions it can't answer?
- Which languages can callers use?
- Can we control data retention and exports, or self-host the software?
- Which costs sit outside the published allowance, and do you bill short or spam calls?

## Virtual receptionist FAQ

### Is a virtual receptionist the same as a virtual assistant?

No. A virtual assistant is a remote worker who does wider admin work, such as email, scheduling and documents, and you often pay one by the hour. A virtual receptionist focuses on your phone calls: answering, booking, taking messages and transferring.

### Is a virtual receptionist legit?

Yes. Virtual receptionists are an established service category, so judge the specific company by its published prices, contract terms and trial. [SAS](https://www.specialtyansweringservice.net/pricing/) and [PATLive](https://www.patlive.com/pricing/) offer 14-day free trials, [Ruby](https://www.ruby.com/plans-and-pricing/) offers first-time customers a money-back guarantee, and LobbyStack's Free plan doesn't expire.
