## The best live answering services for property managers

These six put people on tenant calls at any hour. Each one says it separates emergencies from routine requests using rules you write.

### PATLive: best for software sync with live agents

PATLive's [property management page](https://www.patlive.com/industries/property-management) says agents use "your customized triage rules" to find true emergencies and dispatch your on-call technicians. They either dispatch through your property management software or forward the details to your on-call team. The page names Yardi and AppFolio and shows Rent Manager, Propertyware and Buildium logos. Its FAQ says PATLive can "route by caller type so owners reach you and tenants get the maintenance line."

The [pricing page](https://www.patlive.com/pricing/) lists 11 tiers, from $49 a month plus $2.99 a minute up to 5,000 minutes. PATLive bills the first minute in full, then 6-second steps, and doesn't bill after-call notes. Nights and holidays cost the listed price, there's a 14-day trial, and there's no contract. Spanish costs nothing extra from 8am to 10pm ET.

### MAP Communications: best for emergencies that need a confirmed handoff

MAP's [property management page](https://www.mapcommunications.com/industries/property-management/) says urgent matters go to your on-call maintenance team, and its emergency hotline keeps escalating until your on-call person confirms they got the message. You define what counts as an emergency; the page gives lockouts, water leaks and security issues as examples. Agents also answer prospects' application questions and tenants' rent-payment questions. The page names no property management software and says MAP will work toward a solution if yours isn't connected.

Plans run from $49 a month plus $1.37 a minute to $649 for 500 minutes, with overage falling to $1.28 ([pricing page](https://www.mapcommunications.com/pricing/)). Every plan includes English and Spanish. MAP's [terms](https://www.mapcommunications.com/terms/) (March 2026) round calls up to whole minutes, count after-call work, add a $0.024 telecom fee per call and charge a holiday fee on seven holidays without stating the amount.

### Specialty Answering Service: best per-second billing

Specialty Answering Service (SAS) bills by the second, from $44 a month plus $1.54 a minute, with $0 setup and no contract ([pricing page](https://www.specialtyansweringservice.net/pricing/)). The trial runs 14 days or 200 minutes with no card. Its [property management page](https://www.specialtyansweringservice.net/industries/real-estate/property-management-answering-service/) says agents screen calls "for emergency and non-emergency response" under your protocols, reach on-call staff by phone, text, pager or email, and take on-call schedule changes. The page names no property management software. Patching a caller through to you costs $0.10 a minute.

### Ambs Call Center: best low overage with an on-call portal

Ambs's [property management page](https://www.ambscallcenter.com/property-management-answering-service) says "a flood or a lockout goes straight to your on-call staff, while a routine HVAC request waits its turn." You change the on-call schedule yourself in the myAmbs portal. Agents also qualify leasing prospects and book showings. The page names LeadSimple, PropertyBase and Salesforce, plus Zapier, but not AppFolio, Buildium, Yardi or Rent Manager.

Plans start at $149 for 100 minutes with $1.29 overage and drop to $1.20 a minute on the 2,500-minute plan ([pricing page](https://www.ambscallcenter.com/answering-service-pricing)). Ambs bills in 30-second steps, includes English and Spanish, and charges no holiday fees. It also charges $85 for setup and counts solicitor calls and hang-ups against your minutes. Its terms mention a minimum contract period without stating the length, so ask.

### Continental Message Solution: best for Rent Manager users who want live agents

Rent Manager's [integrations directory](https://www.rentmanager.com/integrations/continental-message-solution/) lists Continental Message Solution (CMS). That listing says "tenant information is pulled automatically" and "maintenance tickets are created in real time," with true emergencies dispatched to your on-call maintenance staff. CMS's own [property management article](https://www.continentalmessage.com/blog/best-property-management-answering-services/) (updated August 21, 2026) says its agents work inside Yardi, AppFolio or Buildium.

CMS bills every 28 days: $50 with no included minutes, $150 for 100, $300 for 250 and $575 for 500 ([pricing page](https://www.continentalmessage.com/pricing/)). Smith.ai's [property management page](https://smith.ai/industries/property-management-answering-service) separates true emergencies ("gas leaks, flooding, no heat") from same-day needs and routes urgent calls to your on-call maintenance staff. ## How should an answering service handle leaks, no heat and lockouts?

It should follow a written list you give it, and the live services here all say they work from one. Your list decides which calls wake your on-call tech and which wait for the morning. Write it before you talk to vendors, then hand the same list to each one.

| Tenant call | Common rule | What to collect |
|---|---|---|
| Active leak, flooding or sewage backup | Emergency: reach the on-call tech now | Unit, where the water is coming from, whether it's spreading to other units |
| No heat in cold weather | Emergency below an indoor temperature you set | Unit, indoor temperature, whether the whole building is out |
| Lockout | Your policy: on-call staff, a locksmith or a fee | Unit, ID check steps, callback number |
| No hot water in one unit | Next business day, unless the whole building is out | Unit, how long it's been out |
| Dripping faucet or broken appliance | Work order for the morning | Unit, description, entry permission |
| Noise complaint | Log it for the office | Unit, time, nature of the noise |

Gas smells, fires and carbon monoxide alarms go to 911 or the gas utility first. Your protocol should say so, and a test call should confirm the service follows it. For wording, see our [phone answering script](/blog/phone-answering-script/) guide.

## How do you choose a property management answering service?

### 1. Count after-hours calls and units

Pull last January's and last July's after-hours call counts and average length from your phone bill. Per-minute plans need the minutes, per-call plans the count, and Buildium the unit count. Price both months, because a cold snap and a heat wave send different volumes.

### 2. Write your emergency list first

Use the table above as a start. Add your lockout policy, the indoor temperature that makes no heat an emergency, and who's on call each night. A written list lets each vendor quote and test against the same rules.

### 3. Place three test calls before you sign

Call each finalist three times: a 2 a.m. leak spreading to the unit below, an owner asking about last month's statement, and a prospect asking about pets in Spanish. Check who answered, what the summary said, and whether the on-call tech got the leak. Trials: PATLive 14 days, SAS 14 days or 200 minutes, MAP free trial, Rosie 7 days, Smith.ai's AI Free plan, and LobbyStack Free, with 30 minutes and a US or Canadian number free for 14 days.

### 4. Read the billing terms

A 20-second wrong number bills as a full minute at MAP and PATLive and as 30 seconds at Ambs. SAS bills it by the second and LobbyStack counts 20 seconds. Smith.ai bills per call and excludes spam, and Goodcall doesn't count wrong numbers. Then check setup, holiday and telecom fees, and notice periods. CMS bills every 28 days, so you get 13 bills a year.

### 5. Keep your number with call forwarding

Forward your existing office number from your carrier after hours, on no answer, or for every call, so the number tenants already have stays the same. Our guide to [forwarding business calls](/blog/how-to-forward-business-calls/) covers the carrier settings. With LobbyStack, your forwarding rule decides which calls it answers.
