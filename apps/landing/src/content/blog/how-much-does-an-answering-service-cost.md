## How much does an answering service cost per month?

For 40 three-minute calls a month, live plans cost $127 to $720. Most land between $127 and $415. For every bill on this page, we take the cheapest plan, or plan plus overage, that covers the minutes. If a vendor publishes no overage rate, we use its cheapest plan that covers the full volume.

### What pushes the price up

- **Call count and length.** Minutes are the billing unit at 13 of the 15 services. On a per-minute rate, a 4-minute average call costs a third more than a 3-minute one.
- **Rounding and billable time.** Whole-minute billing can add more than a third to your talk time, and some vendors also bill hold time or after-call notes (see [how answering services count a minute](#how-answering-services-count-a-minute)).
- **Holidays.** Most vendors here charge the same rate at night, but a few add holiday fees.
- **Add-ons and patching.** Smith.ai charges $1.50 more per call for booking. Patching a caller through to you costs $0.10 a minute at Specialty Answering Service and $0.06 at Ambs Call Center.

## Is it cheaper to pay per minute or per call?

A per-call plan costs less when your average call runs longer than its price per call divided by the per-minute rate you'd pay instead. Against PATLive's 100-minute plan, Smith.ai's $10 calls win past about 5.3 minutes. A Message Center's $2.90 calls win past about 1.8 to 2.8 minutes against per-minute rates of $1.05 to $1.59.

### The break-even call length

1. Take the price per call: $10 on Smith.ai's Starter plan ($300 for 30 calls).
2. Take the per-minute rate you'd pay instead: $1.89 on PATLive's 100-minute plan ($189 for 100 minutes).
3. Divide the first by the second: $10 / $1.89 = 5.3 minutes.
4. If your average call runs longer than that, the per-call plan costs less.

| Per-call price | Per-minute price | Break-even call length |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Smith.ai Starter, $10 a call | PATLive 100-minute plan, $1.89 a minute | About 5.3 minutes |
| Smith.ai Starter, $10 a call | Ruby 100-minute plan, $3.95 a minute | About 2.5 minutes |
| A Message Center Standard, $2.90 a call | AnswerNet Silver $1.05 to SAS 100-minute $1.59 a minute | About 1.8 to 2.8 minutes |

Per-call pricing can be the cheap option. At 50 three-minute calls, A Message Center's Standard plan costs $145, and the cheapest per-minute bill for the same 150 minutes is $160 at AnswerNet.

### Why the smallest plan costs the most per minute

At most vendors, bundles get cheaper per minute as they grow. Ruby drops from $5.00 a minute on 50 minutes to $3.45 on 500, Moneypenny from $3.30 to $1.97, and PATLive from $1.98 to $1.52.

Pay-as-you-go plans charge a base fee plus every minute: AnswerNet $29 plus $1.15, AnswerFirst $30 plus $1.60 to $1.95, SAS $44 plus $1.54, The Perfect Answer $47 plus $1.37 and PATLive $49 plus $2.99. At 30 minutes a month, you'd pay AnswerNet $63.50 with the base fee, or $2.12 a minute.

### How answering services count a minute

Two vendors with the same rate can send different bills because each rounds calls its own way. The table bills one call of 2 minutes and 10 seconds under each rule.

| Billing rule | Vendors | One 2:10 call bills as | 40 such calls bill as |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Whole minute, rounded up | Ruby, The Perfect Answer, MAP, Answering Service Care | 3 minutes | 120 minutes |
| 30-second steps | Abby Connect, Ambs, Moneypenny | 2.5 minutes | 100 minutes |
| First minute in full, then 6 seconds | PATLive | 2.2 minutes | 88 minutes |
| Per second | SAS, AnswerFirst, Davinci | About 2.17 minutes | About 87 minutes |
| Seconds, no round-up | LobbyStack (ours) | About 2.17 minutes | About 87 minutes |
| Per call | Smith.ai, A Message Center | 1 call | 40 calls |
| Not stated | AnswerNet, Continental | Not stated | Not stated |

By our math, 40 calls of 2:10 add up to about 87 minutes of talk. Whole-minute billing turns that into 120 billed minutes, 38% more.

Some vendors bill more than talk time. Ruby counts hold time. PATLive counts hold time while the receptionist looks something up, but not queue time or after-call notes. MAP and Answering Service Care bill after-call work, and Moneypenny bills up to 180 seconds of wrap-up a call. Ambs doesn't bill hold or ring time.

Spam varies too. Three vendors keep spam off the bill: Smith.ai when Caller ID passes, The Perfect Answer for spam calls, and Ruby for robocalls its filter sends to voicemail. Ambs says solicitor calls and hang-ups use plan minutes. The other vendors don't say.

LobbyStack counts usage in seconds and doesn't round a call up to a minute, so a 90-second call uses 1.5 minutes. Calls under 10 seconds and calls the receptionist ends as spam don't count; a call of 10 seconds or more counts in full.

## How do you estimate your own answering service bill?

Start with four numbers from last month's call history: calls, talk minutes, the after-hours share and the spam count. Decide which calls a person must take. Then price those minutes on a per-minute plan, a per-call plan and a bundle with overage, and add rounding and fees.

### Step 1: Pull last month's call log

From your phone provider's call history, note total calls, total talk minutes, the share that came after hours, and how many were spam or hang-ups.

### Step 2: Mark the calls a person must take

Split routine calls (booking, hours, quotes) from calls that need judgment, like complaints or emergencies. An AI receptionist can take the routine ones. Count the judgment calls to decide whether you need a live service.

### Step 3: Price your minutes on each plan

Run your minutes through a per-minute plan, a per-call plan and a bundle with overage. The [break-even formula](#the-break-even-call-length) shows which model suits your average call length.

### Step 4: Add rounding and fees

Under whole-minute billing, add about half a minute per call as a rough estimate. Then add setup, holiday, telecom and add-on fees from the [fees table](#what-fees-do-answering-services-charge-on-top-of-the-plan). The [three worked bills](#how-much-does-an-answering-service-cost-for-a-small-business) show the method end to end.

## Is an answering service worth it?

An answering service is worth it when the jobs you win from answered calls bring in more profit than the monthly bill. Multiply the missed calls you'd answer by the share that would book and your profit per job, then compare the result with the plan price for your call volume.

### The worth-it math in one line

**Missed calls × share that would book × profit per job ≥ monthly bill**

In this example, every input is our assumption. Answering the calls you now miss wins you 2 extra jobs a month at $300 profit each, or $600. That covers every live bill in the 40-call row up to $415. It also covers Abby Connect's $599, with $1 left over, but not Ruby's $720. LobbyStack Starter costs $30 for the same calls. Try your own numbers in the [missed call revenue calculator](/missed-call-revenue-calculator/).

### When a live service is the better buy

Pick a live service when every call needs a person, when calls are sensitive or complex, when your callers expect a human, or when you need warm transfers and on-call dispatch.

### When it costs more than it saves

A service loses money when each job is worth too little for a few extra bookings to cover the bill. It also loses money when per-call pricing meets many short, routine calls. The salon's 100 two-minute calls cost $915 on Smith.ai and $215 at AnswerNet.

An operator answers your calls in your business name, takes messages, screens and routes calls, and patches callers through to you or your team. On some plans, operators also book appointments in your calendar or scheduling software.

### Do answering services charge for spam calls?

It depends on the vendor. Most live services in our table bill month to month, with a notice period in place of a long contract.
