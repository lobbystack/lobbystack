## LobbyStack avoids paying for a receptionist network you do not need

Smith.ai's live-agent network creates real value for a company with nobody available to take a difficult call. Buyers should confirm which escalations the plan includes and which live-agent verification or scheduling tasks add a per-call charge.

Many small businesses already have an owner, dispatcher, or on-call employee for urgent work. LobbyStack handles routine questions and bookings, then transfers the calls that need judgment to that person with context. The Free plan lets the team test 30 minutes of those calls before paying.

LobbyStack also keeps the workflow inspectable. Teams can control providers, retention, prompts, and infrastructure instead of waiting for an enterprise tier to unlock deeper customization.

## Smith.ai sells managed coverage, not only software

Smith.ai has spent years building receptionist operations. Its AI product advertises intake, qualification, scheduling, warm routing, call recording, transcription, CRM connections, and testing through Quality Studio. A business can add live human involvement when a call needs judgment or empathy.

That service model fits law firms, consultancies, and other businesses where a difficult call may need a trained person outside the customer's own staff. It can also reduce the work of building an escalation roster.

LobbyStack transfers a call to the people you configure. It provides context through transcripts, summaries, caller details, and notifications, but it does not include a third-party receptionist workforce. Your staff or chosen answering partner owns the human conversation.

## The price includes different labor models

Smith.ai lists 25 calls on its free tier. Pro starts at $150 for 75 calls, with a published $2 base rate per call. Enterprise starts at $500 for 300 calls, with a published $1.67 base rate per call.

LobbyStack lists 30 free voice minutes, 150 minutes for $30, and 500 minutes for $100. Usage beyond the allowance follows the plan's minute pricing.

Smith.ai costs more at the published entry points, but a pure price comparison misses its service layer. Live-agent capacity, managed onboarding, and a mature receptionist operation carry cost. LobbyStack lowers the software and voice entry price while asking your team to own escalation.

Estimate three numbers:

1. monthly calls and average call length
2. the share of calls that need a person
3. the staff cost of taking those transfers

Those figures show whether a managed hybrid service or an AI system connected to your team costs less.

## Customization and ownership separate the products

Smith.ai's public pricing places custom AI prompting and unlimited custom workflows in Enterprise. A buyer with standard intake and routing can use lower tiers. A firm with unusual rules should confirm the exact plan.

LobbyStack publishes its code. A technical team can inspect the call path, change workflows, bring its own provider accounts in a self-hosted deployment, and set retention controls. The tradeoff is engineering and operations work.

Smith.ai removes more of that work. LobbyStack removes more of the platform boundary.

## A law-firm intake example

A family law firm gets routine consultation requests, court-date questions, urgent calls from existing clients, and emotional first-time inquiries.

Smith.ai can suit the firm if it wants access to an external receptionist after the AI qualifies the call. The firm should ask how agents receive context, which calls trigger a handoff, and what each live-agent charge includes.

LobbyStack can suit the firm if employees must handle all sensitive conversations and the AI should only collect approved intake, schedule consultations, or transfer urgent callers. Self-hosting may also matter when the firm sets strict data-retention and access policies.

Both systems need careful instructions. The AI should not give legal advice, promise an outcome, or expose client information. A live human should follow the same limits.
